Town of Maple Plain • Cumberland, Wisconsin area
Independent community resource. Verify dates and filing requirements with the Town Clerk and Wisconsin Department of Revenue. This is informational, not legal advice.

For a Nov. 7 meeting beginning at 10:00 a.m., the 48-hour point is Thursday, Nov. 5 at 10:00 a.m. Earlier is safer. Confirm receipt with the Clerk.

2. What to do now

  • Put the old assessment, 2026 assessment notice, tax records, and parcel information in one folder.
  • Request the assessor's property record card and compare it with the actual property.
  • Ask which comparable sales, sales analysis, and valuation method support the new value.
  • Write down every contact attempt: date, method, person contacted, response, and records provided.
  • Start gathering market evidence before Open Book rather than waiting for Board of Review.
  • Protect Board of Review filing rights even if the assessor has not responded.

3. Check the assessor's property facts

Land
  • Acreage
  • Water frontage
  • Shoreline quality
  • Slope and usable area
  • Road/access conditions
  • Wetlands, easements, restrictions
Structures and utilities
  • Building square footage
  • Year built and condition
  • Finished areas and outbuildings
  • Running water / well
  • Septic
  • Heat and electric
  • Seasonal vs. year-round use

4. Questions to ask at Open Book

  • What recent sales support my land value?
  • Which properties were treated as comparable to mine?
  • How were differences in frontage, lot size, slope, utilities, structures, access, and condition adjusted?
  • Was a recent arm's-length sale of my property considered?
  • What valuation method was used if adequate comparable sales were unavailable?
  • May I have a copy of my property record card and the sales analysis supporting the assessment?
  • If a factual error is found, when will the corrected value appear on the assessment roll?

5. Build a defensible opinion of value

Your formal objection should identify the value you believe is correct. Strong evidence can include:

  • A recent arm's-length sale of your property.
  • Recent sales of genuinely comparable properties.
  • A professional appraisal, if available.
  • Documented factual errors in the assessor's property record.
  • Photos and records showing meaningful differences from the assessor's comparables.
Lake-property reminder: A nearby sale is not automatically comparable. Consider lake/water body, frontage, usable acreage, slope, access, utilities, septic, structure quality, seasonal use, privacy, and restrictions.

6. Board of Review filing checklist

  • Give the Clerk notice of intent to object at least 48 hours before the first scheduled BOR meeting.
  • Complete the Wisconsin PA-115A Objection Form for Real Property Assessment.
  • State your opinion of value for land and improvements as required on the form.
  • Provide the information requested on the objection form completely and accurately.
  • Keep a copy of everything submitted and proof of delivery/receipt.
  • Bring copies of supporting evidence to the hearing.
  • Be ready to explain the assessed value, your requested value, and the evidence supporting your requested value.

9. Hearing outline

Keep your presentation simple:
  1. My property is currently assessed at $__________.
  2. I believe the correct market value is $__________.
  3. The strongest evidence supporting that value is ______________________________.
  4. The assessor's comparables or property facts differ from my property in these important ways: ______________________________.
  5. I am asking the Board to change the assessment to $__________.

Prepared by the Maple Plain Assessment Project as a community educational resource. Because assessment procedures can change and individual facts matter, verify all deadlines and filing requirements with the Town Clerk and official Wisconsin DOR guidance.
