The formal process

Challenge the value with evidence, not just the percentage increase.

A large increase is a reason to investigate. The strongest appeal explains why the January 1 market value is lower than the assessment and supports that position with facts.

Step by step

How to challenge a Wisconsin property assessment

Treat this like a short evidence case. Keep dates, forms, records, comparable sales, photographs, and contact notes together from the beginning.

Verify the property facts

Ask for the property record card and compare it with reality. Check acreage, waterfront/frontage, square footage, building type, year built, condition, finished areas, outbuildings, water, septic, heat, utilities, seasonal versus year-round use, access, easements, and other material characteristics.

Attend Open Book and ask the assessor to show the work

Ask for the property record card, sales analysis, comparable sales used, and the adjustments that led to the final value. Ask specifically how lake frontage, lot size, slope, utilities, improvements, seasonal use, access, and condition were handled.

Decide your opinion of value

The formal objection form asks for your opinion of the property's value. Build a defensible number from the strongest evidence available, such as a recent arm's-length sale, genuinely comparable sales, a professional appraisal, or documented errors in the assessor's property data.

Give notice of intent to object

Wisconsin DOR states that the owner must give the Board of Review clerk written or oral notice of intent to file an objection at least 48 hours before the first scheduled BOR meeting. Written notice is easier to document.

Complete Form PA-115A

Complete every applicable section of the state-prescribed Objection Form for Real Property Assessment, including your requested value. Keep a copy and proof of delivery.

Present the evidence at Board of Review

The assessor's value begins with a presumption of correctness. State the assessed value, your requested value, and the evidence supporting your number. Bring copies and be prepared to answer questions under oath.

At Open Book

Ask for records, not reassurance.

Useful questions include:

  • What recent sales support this land value?
  • Which properties were treated as comparable?
  • How were differences in frontage, slope, lot size, utilities, access, and structures adjusted?
  • Was a recent arm's-length sale of this property considered?
  • What valuation method was used if comparable sales were unavailable?
  • Can I receive a copy of my property record card and the sales analysis supporting this value?
After Board of Review

If you still disagree

Additional appeal routes may exist, but deadlines and eligibility rules are strict. Do not assume you can skip Board of Review and preserve every later option.

Wisconsin DOR appeal

Wisconsin DOR's property-owner guide explains a state appeal route under Wis. Stat. § 70.85 for qualifying cases. The filing deadline is generally 20 days after the BOR determination, and statutory eligibility limits apply.

Circuit court review

Certiorari review examines the Board of Review record. New evidence generally cannot simply be added later, which makes the record you build at BOR extremely important.

Excessive assessment claim

Wisconsin law also contains an excessive-assessment claim process under Wis. Stat. § 74.37. Because later options interact with BOR procedures, consider legal advice for a substantial dispute.
